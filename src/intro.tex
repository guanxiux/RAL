Dense convolution dominates per-frame latency in vision tasks where reaction
time decides the outcome~\cite{nanoflownet,fastdepth,perclatency}, such as a
car avoiding a pedestrian stepping out of occlusion~\cite{avarch}, a drone
dodging an obstacle~\cite{eventavoid}, or a robot returning a table-tennis
ball~\cite{robottabletennis} (Figure~\ref{fig:teaser}). Yet on a given input
much of that computation is spatially redundant. A
common remedy is masked convolution: an upstream policy marks spatially
active positions on the dense feature grid, and a dedicated sparse
convolution operator updates only these positions in the dense output. The mask may come from event-camera increments~\cite{evconv,asyncsparse}, temporal
differences~\cite{deltacnn,skipconv}, or learned spatial
gates~\cite{dynconv,dgnet}. These masks promise large reductions in
convolution work, yet existing masked-convolution
systems~\cite{evconv,deltacnn,dgnet,dynconv} rarely turn the sparsity into proportional latency reduction.

Dense GPU convolution obtains high \emph{throughput} (operations per second)
from tiled execution: each tile enumerates a contiguous block of output
coordinates and reduces over channels and kernel offsets along a regular
datapath (the dense reduction datapath) with coalesced loads and
weight-tile reuse.
In masked convolution, reducing latency proportionally requires sustaining
this throughput while achieving a high \emph{useful-compute ratio} (the
fraction of issued operations that produce needed outputs), but the mask
places the two axes in tension: it selects scattered positions while each
tile covers a contiguous block, so keeping the blocks issues operations on
inactive positions, and abandoning them gives up this datapath.

Neither existing execution paradigm sustains both axes.
\emph{Gather-scatter}~\cite{sai,dgnet,dynconv} selects exactly the active
positions but gives up the dense reduction datapath: it extracts them into a
packed representation, computes, and scatters the results back, adding memory
passes that materialize their receptive fields and a host synchronization per
layer. \emph{Tile skipping}~\cite{sbnet,skipconv,evconv,deltacnn} keeps the
dense reduction datapath but selects positions only per tile: it suppresses
entirely inactive tiles and wastes computation on partially active ones.
On an RTX~3080 running YOLOv8m~\cite{yolov8} at batch~4 under temporal-difference
masks~\cite{deltacnn}, 82.3\% of convolution work is unnecessary
(an \emph{active ratio} of 17.7\%).
Gather-scatter sustains only 31.4\% of the dense pipeline's throughput,
reducing latency by just 43.8\%.
Tile skipping sustains 41.9\% but
wastes 33.1\% of issued operations, reducing latency by only 36.9\%,
slower than gather-scatter despite its throughput advantage
(\S\ref{sec:bg:gap}).

We observe that output coordinates can serve as the interface between
position selection and the dense reduction datapath, resolving this
tension. Because the input stays on the dense grid, every neighbor lies at
a fixed offset from an output's base address, so a worklist of active
positions in place of a tile's contiguous coordinate block sets only
per-row base addresses and leaves the reduction loop intact. We characterize this
\emph{worklist-driven tiled execution} on a desktop and an embedded GPU
(\S\ref{sec:char}) and find that it preserves the per-tile throughput of
dense execution: a worklist tile executes the same number of instructions as a dense
tile and spends 0.98--1.04$\times$ its SM cycles. This parity carries over
to a whole layer when the tile configuration follows the worklist length:
convolution time is then affine in the active ratio with a slope matching
the dense marginal cost, and the residual gap is an intercept of
5.6--17.4\% of dense time per layer (median per GPU and precision),
dominated by kernel launch latency and fixed per-tile stalls, not by
throughput loss.

While worklist-driven execution retains dense throughput within each tile,
two challenges remain in the full per-frame path.
First, \emph{worklist construction cost}: deriving the worklist from the
mask requires scanning the whole grid and costs time nearly independent of
the active count, so construction's share of latency grows as activity falls
and accumulates when every layer rebuilds. At an active ratio of 17.5\%,
rebuilding at every layer adds 2.6--18.7\% to
convolution time (\S\ref{sec:char:bound}).
Second, \emph{worklist consumption efficiency}: the best tile configuration
shifts with the worklist length, but
the length is visible only on the device after construction and
changes across frames and layers. Reading it back to the host stalls the
per-frame path; adapting on the device avoids the stall but incurs a
non-negligible cost per decision point, as current GPUs lack a lightweight
mechanism to condition a launch on a device-resident value. Even the cheapest
existing mechanism costs more than it saves on 7--25\% of YOLOv8m layers (\S\ref{sec:char:cost}).

We present \sysname
(\textbf{W}orklist-dr\textbf{I}ven ma\textbf{S}k\textbf{E}d
\textbf{Conv}o\-lu\-tion), which drives the unchanged dense pipeline with
a worklist of active outputs and co-designs worklist construction and
consumption to meet both challenges.
To reduce construction cost, a single mask-space pass fuses output-mask
propagation with per-tile compaction, operating on only mask and coordinate
metadata; compatible operators reuse the worklist instead of
reconstructing it.
To sustain consumption efficiency, a persistent compute grid consumes the
worklist without host synchronization, and a cost-aware selection mechanism
adapts the tile configuration per frame, adding a candidate configuration to a layer only
where its expected gain exceeds the selection overhead.

This paper makes the following contributions:
\begin{itemize}
  \item We characterize worklist-driven tiled execution on two GPUs with
        hardware counters, explaining why its tiles match dense tiles,
        locating its remaining gap, and identifying the hardware support that
        would narrow it (\S\ref{sec:char}).

  \item We present \sysname, which co-designs the worklist's construction and consumption around these findings to sustain both throughput and useful-compute ratio,
        narrowing the sparsity-to-speedup gap in masked convolution.

  % geomean over 44 workload-device-precision cases (fastest available baseline, dense included), 2026-11-16
  \item We evaluate \sysname on six GPUs and four workloads, where it
        achieves 2.06$\times$ (FP32) and 2.09$\times$ (FP16) geometric-mean
        speedups over the fastest baseline per workload-platform pair and
        reduces energy by 60.8--82.5\% versus dense
        execution on embedded GPUs (\S\ref{sec:eval}).
\end{itemize}
