\title{\sysname: Closing the Sparsity-to-Speedup Gap in Masked Convolution with Worklist-Driven Execution}
\author{Anonymous Authors}

\begin{abstract}
% v15 (ISCA framing, 2026-11-07)
Masked convolution promises to reduce inference latency by computing only
positions an upstream policy marks active, yet existing paths sacrifice either
throughput or useful-compute ratio, failing to translate sparsity into
proportional latency reduction. We find that redirecting a dense tiled
pipeline to compute only scattered active positions largely preserves per-tile
throughput: irregularity is confined to only row-level indirection outside the
reduction loop, leaving channel-contiguous access and weight-tile reuse
identical to dense execution. Based on this finding, we present \sysname,
which drives the dense tiled pipeline with an active-output worklist to
achieve both high useful-compute ratio and high throughput. To handle a
construction cost that does not shrink with sparsity, \sysname builds the
worklist in a single mask-space pass and reuses it across compatible operators.
To adapt tile configurations as the workload size shifts per frame on the
device, \sysname introduces on-device tile switching that trades off each
candidate's gain against its selection cost. Across
six GPUs and four workloads, \sysname achieves a XX$\times$
geometric-mean latency reduction over the fastest baseline per
workload-platform pair, reducing energy by XX--XX\% on embedded GPUs.
\end{abstract}
