\begin{abstract}
% ORIG (RAL/robotics framing):
% Onboard robotic perception must sustain high update rates under tight compute
% and power budgets. Masking policies from event increments, temporal frame
% differences, and learned gates expose where computation is unnecessary, yet
% existing masked-convolution paths do not convert this sparsity into proportional
% latency reduction.
Masked convolution accelerates grid-structured vision models by evaluating only
the positions an upstream policy marks active. Yet existing masked-convolution
paths do not convert this sparsity into proportional latency reduction. Tile skipping runs the dense pipeline while skipping empty tiles, keeping
throughput high but computing inactive positions within retained tiles, suffering
a low useful-compute ratio; gather-scatter raises the useful-compute ratio to
near one but loses throughput to feature materialization, irregular access, and
% ORIG word "runtime": an execution model
writeback. We present \sysname, a runtime for masked convolution that fuses
selectivity into the dense pipeline to achieve both a high useful-compute ratio
and high throughput. \sysname constructs an active-output worklist,
which drives the dense pipeline to compute only the active positions. To bound the
overhead of building this worklist and to keep the GPU full despite its short,
input-dependent length, \sysname co-designs its construction and consumption:
it reuses the constructed worklist across compatible operators, emits the worklist in dense
traversal order, and decomposes work adaptively over the spatial and reduction
dimensions. Across
four perception models and six GPU platforms, \sysname reduces full-model latency
by 22--46\% relative to the fastest competing path and per-frame energy by
28--60\% over dense convolution on the embedded platforms.
\end{abstract}
